# Analysis: Mollick (2014) margins on the September 2026 crawl

Run on 3 October 2026 by the reference runner (host mode, no image), twice: under the seeds the archive issued for the two receipts. The input (268,088,678 bytes, SHA-256 `b3df9668…0b45`) was fetched from webrobots.io's S3 bucket and verified by hash before each run; the second run used the runner's verified cache. Sixteen of the eighteen outputs are identical under both seeds; the two bootstrap standard errors differ, as they should.

**Sample.** 194,160 rows, 157,331 distinct projects, 138,189 decided campaigns (45,981 failed, 92,208 successful; success rate 66.7%, against Mollick's 48.1% in 2009–2012: the crawl is not the universe of projects, and Kickstarter's own curation and the mix of categories have changed). Launches from 2009, deadlines to 2026. Computed `pledged/goal` agreed with Kickstarter's `percent_funded` to within one point on 100% of kept projects.

**P1, failures fail by large margins: holds.** Mean pledged/goal among failed projects 0.0965 (Mollick 10.3%); 11.07% of failed projects raised 30% or more of their goal (Mollick 10%; bootstrap SE 0.15 points); 4.48% raised 50% or more (Mollick 3%). All three well inside the pre-registered bounds (0.20, 0.06, 0.20). A decade on, the shape of failure is almost exactly as Mollick described it. **Claim A: confirmed.**

**P2, successes succeed by small margins: fails.** The 25th percentile of over-funding is 5.84% (Mollick 3%; bound 6%, so this half holds, narrowly), but the median successful project is **24.7% over its goal** (bootstrap SE 0.23 points; Mollick "about 10%"; bound 20%). Only 16.5% of successful projects finish within 3% of goal (Mollick 25%) and 33.6% within 10% (Mollick 50%). Successful campaigns now overshoot far more than they did in 2012, which is what the pre-registration expected to be at risk (stretch goals and pre-order campaigns). **Claim B: failed**, by the test registered with it (median of pledged/goal 1.2466 > 1.20).

Both bundles declare the crawl as an inputs/0.1 input (access open); no cross-check was assigned, as these are the first receipts on their claims. Outputs are withheld by the archive until a cross-check matches or thirty days pass; the two outputs files here are the runs' own records.

**Limitations.** A monthly crawl, not the universe of projects; one crawl date; margins in the project's own currency without inflation or currency adjustment (as Mollick); no image pinned, so determinism was not observed and the receipts cannot carry a finding; the operator is at the account tier, so these receipts move credence but cannot resolve the claims until a verified operator checks them.
